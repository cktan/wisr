# soak run 20261109

First soak of digres under the school workload of
[`SOAK.md`](../SOAK.md). It looks for a memory leak and for
slowing over time.

- Machine: Neoverse-V2, 4 cores, 15 GB, Linux 7.3.0-1004-gcp
- digres: 5bd4f637, `make -C dg release`; `digres --build` says:

      digres   -O2 -DNDEBUG
      sq       -O2 -DNDEBUG
      sm       -O3 -DNDEBUG
      RocksDB  9.11.2, static, -O3 -DNDEBUG

- PostgreSQL: 18.6 (autovacuum on, `jit = off`, no parallel
  workers). Its roles log in by SCRAM, as digres's do.
- Server on CPUs 0 and 1, clients on CPUs 2 and 3.
- Clients: 5 that reconnect for every transaction (3 student, 2
  prof) and 4 that stay connected (3 student, 1 prof).
- Mix measured: 82% read, 6.4% update, 6% delete, 6% insert.
  The update share is under its 10% goal: the grade update is
  slow, so the clients run it less.
- Each run has 15 minutes of warm-up that the tables below do
  not count. The tables use `soak/report.py` with 900 s.

## Findings

### 1. No slowing in 4 hours (dg-2gb)

- Total tps rose 0.6%. The p99 latency of the persistent
  clients moved by 1% to 3%.
- The data directory is a sawtooth: it grows to 6 GB, then falls
  to 1 GB when the reapers and compaction run, about every 100
  minutes. The peaks of the two halves are equal.
- Threads and descriptors settle in the first two hours and stay.

### 2. The server's memory grows, and the cause is not known

- In dg-2gb, PSS rose from 404 to 1682 MB with no plateau, at 270
  to 430 MB an hour. PostgreSQL stayed at 120 MB.
- With 256 MB of shared buffers, PSS grows 270, 160 and then 137
  MB an hour in three half-hours. The rate falls, and the size at
  the end (583 MB) is 90 MB over what the setting alone explains
  (236 MB at start plus 256 MB).
- So most of the growth follows `shared_buffers`: the buffer
  pool fills as pages are touched. The 2 GB run ended at 1.7 GB,
  still under its 2 GB, so it may only be filling.
- Not shown: whether a residual 100 to 140 MB an hour is a leak.
  A run of 8 hours or more at 256 MB would show it. If PSS
  stays near 500 to 600 MB, there is no leak.
- The p99 of the persistent clients in dg-256mb rose 11% and
  18% (6.27 to 6.95, 5.50 to 6.47 ms). The first and last
  tenths fall in different parts of the sawtooth, so this may
  be that and not slowing. This was not tested.

### 2b. Follow-up (20261110): update-only runs and rel

- A 3-hour run of only the grade update (256 MB, `MIX=updateonly`)
  gave PSS 234 MB at start, 606 MB after 1 hour, 688 MB after
  3 hours: 47 MB an hour in the second half, no drop yet. Tps
  and p99 stayed flat (+1.3%, 6.13 to 6.07 ms). All end and
  restart checks passed.
- The same updates through `rel`, with no SQL (`leakrel.rel`,
  `leakrel2.rel`), also grow. Valgrind memcheck finds 0 bytes
  lost at exit. Massif shows that only the RocksDB memtable
  arena grows; the other allocation sites stay fixed. See
  [`../LEAK.md`](../LEAK.md).
- So the growth in `rel` is live memtable data, not a leak.
  Not shown: that digres stops growing. Its column families
  are more, so its ceiling is higher.

### 3. A null index key reads the whole index (found, not fixed)

Found while the workload was built: a delete took 25 ms and grew
slower as the table grew. Reproduced and written up as issue 28
in [`dg/ISSUE.md`](../../dg/ISSUE.md). The workload uses
`class_id IN (subquery)` to avoid it.

### 4. The workload shrinks the table (a flaw in the workload)

The table fell from 100,000 to about 78,000 rows on all three
runs, PostgreSQL included. Inserts succeed less often than
deletes. The size was still falling at the end, so the data does
not reach a steady state; the sizes above are of a table that
shrinks.

### 5. digres aborts far fewer transactions than PostgreSQL

The add class runs `SERIALIZABLE`. In 76 M transactions digres
retried 1,091; PostgreSQL retried 53,196 in 62 M. The capacity
check holds on both.

Examined 20261110: digres aborts the conflicts it must, and no
others.

- A conflict needs two adds to the same class at the same time.
  About 6% of the transactions are adds: 284 a second, each
  open about 2 ms, so about 0.57 adds are open at a time. The
  chance that another add is open on the same class is
  0.57 / 2,500, about 0.023%. digres retried 1,091 of about
  4.6 M adds, 0.024%.
- PostgreSQL retried 1.4% of its adds. Its SIREAD locks are
  coarser: a btree index is locked by leaf page, which holds the
  entries of several classes, and heap tuple locks become page
  and relation locks past `max_pred_locks_per_page` and
  `max_pred_locks_per_relation`. sm1 locks the key range read.
- Two tests take the add's shape, by btree and by lsm. Two
  sessions add to one class with one seat left: the second
  commit fails. Two sessions add to two classes: both commit.
  `sm/rel/test/test_seat_ssi.rel` does it through `sm.h`;
  `dg/test/seat_ssi.sh` runs `soak/add_class.sql`'s statement,
  ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING included, in `server.sh`.

## Not done

  digres runs, and the descriptor slope on dg-256mb is 1.05
  against a limit of 1. PostgreSQL itself fails the tps and p99
  limits (-7.2%, up to +10%), so the 5% limit is tight for a run
  of this length.
- No ASan or LSan build exists. The leak hunt of `SOAK.md`
  section 9 was not run.
- The 8-hour run at 256 MB of finding 2 was not run.
